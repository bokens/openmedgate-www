*The first episode of the “One bid” series, published on LinkedIn on 21 October 2026, in Polish. This is a translation of the text as it appeared that day, with subheadings and a list of documents.*

One bid. PLN 349,320 against a budget of PLN 350,000. That is how, on 19 October, a procedure I had followed since August was decided; we had asked questions in it. We did not submit a bid. I will explain why in a moment.

## What the hospital was buying

The subject: integration of a hospital information system with the national Central e-Registration service. A regional hospital, EU funds. In September, after our questions, the buyer admitted an equivalent module, described six criteria of equivalence, lowered the participation threshold from PLN 400,000 to PLN 200,000 and extended the deadline. By the book.

## The question about interface documentation

Then we asked one thing: who would provide the documentation of the interfaces of the system we were to integrate with. The answer of 22 September, quoted:

> “The contracting authority does not hold the technical documentation of the HIS interfaces (…) and has no possibility of obtaining it or making it available to contractors, because this documentation is the proprietary documentation of the system's producer.”

The hospital does not hold the interface documentation of its own system. And it has no way of getting it.

## Who pays for the producer's consent

Further: the costs of “consents, the provision of interfaces or the services of the HIS producer” are to be “included by the contractor in the bid price”. And before submitting a bid, the buyer “recommends making the necessary arrangements with the producer of the HIS”.

So the producer decides who may compete. And the price of its consent is paid by the hospital anyway, in the price of the only bid.

Nobody bids on something that cannot be priced. One party did.

## The same question at a larger scale

Today the same question returns at a larger scale. Until 31 October the Polish e-Health Centre is collecting comments on a draft of open communication standards between systems. The draft will regulate the data format: 30 profiles on HL7 FHIR. It will not regulate what decided this procedure: whether a hospital has the right to the description of its system's interfaces, and whether it may give it to a contractor of its own choice.

## Three sentences for every contract

Three sentences I would look for in every contract with the producer of a hospital system:

1. The description of the installation's interfaces is held by the hospital, and the hospital may hand it to a contractor it names.
2. The terms and the price of third-party access to the interfaces are set on the day the contract is signed, not anew at every integration.
3. The hospital decides who gets access to the test environment of its system.

Is there a chance that the first of them becomes an obligation? A public description of the capabilities of every installation? In FHIR that mechanism already exists and is called CapabilityStatement.

Directors, heads of IT: which of these three sentences do you have in your contract? If none, it is not your fault. Nobody required it of you. But now you know what a tender without them looks like.
